# Calibration — coarse presence sensor

## The headline, first

**You cannot convert this sensor's output into a number of people.** Not with a
correction factor, not with a lookup table, not with a fitted curve. The
modelling below shows the unique-hash-to-people ratio moving between **1.4× and
10.1×** under assumptions the sensor has no way to observe — whether phones are
screen-on, and how old the device population is. A conversion factor calibrated
on Tuesday is wrong on Wednesday.

What the sensor is good for is **change**: more radios are probing here than
were probing here a minute ago. That is a real signal and it is the only one
claimed.

---

## Why the count is wrong

A probe request is what a phone broadcasts when it looks for known networks.
Counting distinct source MACs seems like it should count devices. It does not:

1. **MAC randomization.** iOS 8+ and Android 10+ use a fresh random MAC for
   each probe burst by default. One phone in one minute can present a dozen
   different addresses. This inflates the count and is the dominant error.
2. **Devices, not people.** A phone, a watch, a laptop and a car head unit are
   four radios and one person.
3. **Missed bursts.** The sensor hops 11 channels at 180 ms dwell, so it is
   listening on the right channel for roughly 1/11 of the time. Most bursts are
   never heard. This *deflates* the count — in the opposite direction to (1),
   by an unrelated amount.
4. **WiFi off is invisible.** A phone with WiFi disabled, or in airplane mode,
   does not exist to this sensor. In an evacuation, a lot of phones are dead.
5. **Range is not a boundary.** A radio 100 m away through a wall may be heard;
   one 5 m away in a car may not. There is no defined sensing volume.

Errors (1) and (3) do not cancel. They are independent and they move the answer
by different factors under different conditions.

is the whole argument against reporting a people count.

## The low-density problem

At the densities that would matter for "is anyone still in this
neighbourhood?", the sensor is at its worst:

| People | Baseline 90 % range |
|---:|:---:|
| 0 | 0–0 |
| 1 | 0–6 |
| 2 | 1–7 |
| 5 | 6–15 |

One person can read as zero. One person can read as six. **The sensor cannot
distinguish an empty street from a street with a few people on it**, which is
exactly the question someone would most want to ask it. Zero is the only
reading with any confidence, and even that only means "no radios probing" —
not "nobody here."

---

## Saturation

`HASH_SLOTS = 1024`. Sensitivity A reaches 1013 unique hashes at 100 people and
2014 at 200 — past capacity. When the set fills, the firmware sets the
`saturated` flag and prints a warning, and **the reported count becomes a floor,
not a count**. Any window with `saturated=1` must be discarded from analysis or
treated as "≥1024", never as a value.

## Measurement protocol — to produce the real table

The `Measured (fill in)` column is empty because these numbers have not been
measured on hardware. Filling it in honestly:

1. **Site.** One location, fixed sensor position, 60 s windows, ≥ 30 windows per
   density point. Note construction materials and anything metal nearby.
2. **Ground truth.** A human counts people in the defined area per window. Not
   an app, not a turnstile — a person with a tally counter. Record the area
   boundary explicitly; "in range" is not a boundary.
3. **Densities.** 0, 1, 2, 5, 10, 25, 50 people. The 0 and 1 points matter most
   and are the ones most often skipped.
4. **Record covariates**, because they are what move the ratio: approximate
   screen-on fraction, device-age mix, whether people are stationary or moving,
   and temperature (it affects how many phones are dead).
5. **Report the spread, not the mean.** Publish the 5th–95th percentile band per
   density. A single mean ratio implies a precision that does not exist.
6. **Re-run seasonally.** OS updates change randomization behaviour. A
   calibration is valid for a device population, not forever.

A calibration that produces a single conversion factor has been done wrong.

## Privacy and legal properties

Enforced in `device_counter.ino`, not just asserted here:

| Property | How |
|---|---|
| Never transmits | Promiscuous receive-only; `WIFI_MODE_AP` never set; no beacon/probe-response path exists |
| Never impersonates a network | Same — there is no code that could emit a beacon. Rogue-AP operation is a federal crime |
| Never reads payloads | Only the MAC header's subtype and `addr2` are parsed. Intercepting communications content is a federal crime |
| Never captures SSIDs | The probe request's SSID element identifies homes and workplaces. The frame body is never read |
| MACs hashed immediately | Salted SHA-256, truncated to 64 bits; the raw address is zeroed in the callback |
| Unlinkable across windows | The 128-bit salt is regenerated every window, so the same phone hashes differently each window |
| Nothing persists | Hash set wiped at every boundary; nothing written to flash; output is one aggregate integer |

An unsalted hash of a MAC is **not** anonymisation — the 48-bit space is
trivially enumerable. The rotating salt is what makes the stored value safe,
and it is why the firmware cannot track a device over time even if someone
dumps its memory.

**Deployment is a separate question from legality.** Receive-only operation
being lawful does not make it appropriate to deploy anywhere. Use only on
property you control or with the owner's permission, and post signage where
people would not expect to be sensed.
